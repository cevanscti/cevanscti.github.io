Foundations

Precise definitions matter in intelligence work. Ambiguity in terminology leads to misaligned requirements, poor collection decisions, and finished products that don't serve their consumers. These are the working definitions that anchor this practice.

Intelligence

Processed information with purpose

The collection and processing of information about a competitive entity, needed by an organization to ensure its security and well-being. Intelligence is both a product and an encompassing process of methodologies — not raw data.

Threat Intelligence

Analytical rigor applied to adversary behavior

Processed information — refined through analytical rigor — about the hostile intent, capabilities, and opportunities of an adversarial entity, produced to satisfy a structured, defined requirement. Threat intelligence centers on the human threat actor.

Why it matters

The distinction drives everything downstream

Data becomes intelligence only when it has been refined, contextualized, and aligned to a requirement. A raw IOC is not intelligence. A finished product that tells a decision-maker what an adversary is likely to do next — and why — is.

Intelligence Lifecycle

Planning & Direction

Defines the intelligence requirements — the specific questions that must be answered. This phase sets the scope and purpose for everything that follows. Poor direction produces irrelevant intelligence regardless of collection quality.

Collection

Systematic gathering of raw data from OSINT, technical feeds, classified sources, human reporting, and partner sharing. Governed by a Collection Management Framework to ensure consistency and traceability.

Processing & Exploitation

Raw data is normalized, deduplicated, enriched, and structured for analysis. This phase transforms volume into something an analyst can actually work with — without it, analysts drown in noise.

Analysis & Production

The intellectual core of the cycle. Analysts apply tradecraft — structured analytical techniques, adversary modeling, TTP mapping — to produce finished intelligence that answers the stated requirement with confidence levels attached.

Dissemination

Finished intelligence is delivered to the right consumer, in the right format, at the right time. A technically flawless product delivered late or to the wrong audience has no operational value.

Feedback & Re-direction

Consumers evaluate the product's relevance and accuracy. That feedback closes the loop — identifying collection gaps, refining requirements, and improving the next cycle. Intelligence without feedback doesn't improve.

Program Requirements

A functional threat intelligence program requires three foundational elements before any collection or analysis begins. These are not optional enhancements — they are the minimum viable structure for intelligence work that is reproducible, defensible, and operationally useful.

01

Intelligence Requirements

What priority question or task needs to be answered? Without defined requirements, collection is unfocused and analysis produces outputs that no one asked for. Requirements are set by stakeholders and refined through the feedback loop.

02

Threat Model

What framework guides the internal process? A defined threat model reduces process conflicts, aligns team expectations, and grounds analysis in consistent methodology. Without it, every analyst is working from a different mental map.

03

Collection Management Framework

How is information gathered, stored, and utilized throughout the entire process? A CMF governs source selection, data handling, storage, and access — ensuring that collection decisions are deliberate, documented, and auditable.

Operational Functions

Threat intelligence serves three distinct organizational functions. A mature program contributes to all three simultaneously — not as separate workstreams, but as integrated outputs of the same analytical effort.

◉

Preventive

SOC alignment through detection support, alerting, and triage enrichment. Intelligence enables defenders to prioritize the right signals and reduce time-to-detect against known adversary patterns.

▲

Response

IR/IH support through IOC and artifact enrichment. During an active incident, intelligence accelerates attribution, scoping, and containment decisions by providing context that raw telemetry cannot.

◆

Strategic

Business decision support and resource prioritization input. At the strategic level, threat intelligence informs where an organization invests defensively — translating adversary behavior into risk language executives can act on.
