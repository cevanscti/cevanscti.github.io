US Critical Infrastructure
Threat Landscape

A requirement-driven analysis of adversary activity targeting US critical infrastructure over the preceding 12-month period. Produced to demonstrate applied intelligence lifecycle methodology — from defined requirement through finished assessment.

Intelligence Requirement

What is the current threat landscape facing US critical infrastructure, and which adversary actors present the highest risk to energy, water, telecommunications, and government sectors over the preceding 12-month period?

Priority Threat Actors

Three nation-state actors and one Iran-affiliated hacktivist group represent the most active and consequential threats to US critical infrastructure in this assessment period. Each presents a distinct targeting philosophy, operational tempo, and risk profile.

Volt Typhoon

Also tracked as: Bronze Silhouette, Vanguard Panda, DEV-0391, Voltzite

PRC State-Sponsored Energy Comms Water Transportation

Adversary

PRC state-sponsored. Assessed with high confidence to operate on behalf of the People's Liberation Army with strategic pre-positioning objectives, not immediate espionage.

Capability

Advanced LOTL (living-off-the-land) tradecraft. Leverages native OS tools to blend with normal administrator behavior, avoiding detection by signature-based controls. Demonstrated zero-day exploitation and custom botnet infrastructure (KV-Botnet, disrupted Jan 2024 — rebuilt).

Infrastructure

Compromised SOHO routers used as relay nodes to obscure origin traffic. Rebuilt botnet infrastructure rapidly after January 2024 US government disruption, demonstrating high operational resilience.

Victim

US lifeline sector organizations — energy, water, communications, transportation — particularly in Guam and continental US. PRC-backed actors maintained access for months inside a public power utility in Littleton, MA.

MITRE ATT&CK — Selected TTPs

T1078 Valid accounts — uses stolen or compromised legitimate credentials to blend with normal user activity and avoid triggering alerts
T1036 Masquerading — renames tools and processes to mimic legitimate system binaries, evading process-based detection logic
T1059.001 PowerShell — uses native PowerShell for reconnaissance, lateral movement, and data staging, leaving minimal forensic footprint
T1133 External remote services — gains initial access through internet-facing VPN and remote access services, often via unpatched vulnerabilities
T1571 Non-standard ports — routes C2 traffic over non-standard ports to evade perimeter inspection and network-based detection rules

Salt Typhoon

Also tracked as: Earth Estries, GhostEmperor, FamousSparrow, UNC2286

PRC State-Sponsored Telecommunications Defense Government

Adversary

PRC Ministry of State Security (MSS)-affiliated. Targeting pattern and intelligence collection focus align with Chinese strategic intelligence priorities — surveillance capability and pre-positioning against communications infrastructure.

Capability

Demonstrated long-dwell persistence — confirmed access to one telecom network maintained for three years before discovery. Exploited critical vulnerability in Cisco IOS XE. Accessed lawful intercept systems, call records, and subscriber metadata at multiple carriers.

Infrastructure

Compromised at least nine major US telecommunications carriers including AT&T, Verizon, T-Mobile, Lumen, and Charter Communications. By August 2025, FBI confirmed compromise of 200+ organizations across 80 countries.

Victim

Telecom providers, defense contractors, government agencies. A US state Army National Guard network was compromised via weakly configured remote access — yielding administrator credentials, network diagrams, and inter-state connectivity data.

MITRE ATT&CK — Selected TTPs

T1190 Exploit public-facing application — exploited Cisco IOS XE vulnerability for initial access into telecommunications network infrastructure
T1098 Account manipulation — modified privileged accounts to maintain persistent access across environment after initial compromise
T1560 Archive collected data — staged and compressed collected call records and subscriber metadata prior to exfiltration
T1040 Network sniffing — intercepted network traffic to collect credentials and communications metadata at scale across carrier infrastructure
T1083 File and directory discovery — mapped victim network structure and identified high-value data repositories for targeted collection

CyberAv3ngers / IRGC-affiliated APT

Also tracked as: Hydro Kitten, Storm-0784, UNC5691

Iran / IRGC Water / Wastewater Energy OT / ICS

Adversary

Formally affiliated with Iran's Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps. Operationally distinct from PRC actors — goal is disruption and public signaling, not silent persistence. Activity escalated sharply following US-Israel military actions against Iran in 2025-2026.

Capability

Specializes in OT/ICS targeting — PLCs, HMIs, and SCADA systems. Deployed custom malware (IOControl) in 2024 to remotely control water and fuel management systems. Nov 2023–Jan 2024 campaign compromised 75+ Unitronics PLC devices across water and wastewater infrastructure.

Infrastructure

Targets internet-exposed industrial control systems. Exploitation is often low-sophistication — default or absent credentials, publicly known vulnerabilities — but operationally impactful due to the nature of targeted systems. April 2026 joint advisory confirmed active Rockwell Automation CompactLogix and Micro850 PLC exploitation.

Victim

US water and wastewater facilities, energy utilities, government facilities. Confirmed victim: Municipal Water Authority of Aliquippa, Pennsylvania. US sanctioned six IRGC officials in February 2024 in response. Iranian-affiliated attacks spiked 133% in May–June 2025 following US-Israeli strikes.

MITRE ATT&CK — Selected TTPs

T1078.001 Default accounts — exploits PLCs and HMIs with unchanged default credentials, a persistent configuration management failure across OT environments
T1565.002 Transmitted data manipulation — maliciously altered data displayed on HMI and SCADA screens to cause operational confusion and potential physical disruption
T1059 Command and scripting interpreter — used OT-specific diagnostic tools and scripting to interact with PLC project files and manipulate device configurations
T1498 Network denial of service — allied group NoName057(16) conducted coordinated DDoS operations against US infrastructure targets in parallel with OT intrusion campaigns
T1190 Exploit public-facing application — targeted internet-exposed industrial control systems, particularly Rockwell Automation Allen-Bradley PLCs accessible without VPN or firewall protection

Z-Pentest / GRU-affiliated Hacktivists

Allied with: CARR (People's Cyber Army of Russia), NoName057(16)

Russia / GRU OT / ICS Energy Government

Adversary

Established September 2024. Assessed as GRU unit 74455 (Sandworm)-adjacent — GRU likely funded tooling used by allied CARR group. Z-Pentest combines OT intrusion capability with information operations, using claimed attacks to amplify pro-Russia messaging.

Capability

OT intrusion specialization with hack-and-leak and defacement operations. Notably avoids DDoS in favor of OT intrusions for greater media impact. Posted video evidence of HMI defacement operations in March and April 2025. Actively shared TTPs with allied hacktivist groups to scale operational reach.

Infrastructure

Operates via Telegram channels for command coordination and public attribution. Leverages NoName057(16) subscriber base for amplification. Tools funded through GRU unit 74455 through at least September 2024.

Victim

Globally dispersed critical infrastructure — energy, water, and government facilities. US targets selected for geopolitical signaling value. Operations designed to generate media coverage and demonstrate capability rather than achieve silent persistent access.

MITRE ATT&CK — Selected TTPs

T1491.002 External defacement — altered HMI display names and system identifiers to NoName057(16) and CARR references as proof-of-access messaging
T1499 Endpoint denial of service — coordinated with allied groups to conduct service disruption operations against infrastructure targets
T1566 Phishing — used alongside OT intrusion campaigns to gain initial access to IT networks before pivoting toward OT environments
T1485 Data destruction — claimed destructive operations against OT project files as part of hack-and-leak information operations

Trend Analysis

Observed patterns across this assessment period indicate a threat environment that is increasing in both volume and operational sophistication, with three distinct trend lines warranting priority attention.

⇧ Increasing

OT/ICS as a primary target surface

All four actors in this assessment demonstrated active interest in operational technology environments. Iranian actors in particular have shifted from IT-side intrusions toward direct PLC and SCADA manipulation — a meaningful escalation in potential physical consequence.

⇧ Increasing

Geopolitically-triggered attack surges

Iranian-affiliated attacks spiked 133% following US-Israeli military actions in 2025. This pattern — where kinetic geopolitical events directly trigger cyber operation escalation — represents a persistent and predictable risk multiplier for critical infrastructure defenders.

⇧ Increasing

Pre-positioning for disruption, not just espionage

Volt Typhoon's assessed intent — maintaining dormant access for activation during a future crisis — represents a strategic shift from traditional cyber espionage toward infrastructure sabotage capability. 70% of cyberattacks in 2024–2025 targeted critical infrastructure sectors.

~ Persistent

Credential abuse and default configuration failures

Across all actors, exploitation of default credentials, unpatched internet-facing systems, and weak remote access configurations remains the dominant initial access vector. This is not a novel finding — it is a persistent, systemic failure in asset hygiene across critical sectors.

~ Persistent

Long-dwell persistence as operational standard

Salt Typhoon maintained access to one telecom network for three years. PRC actors maintained access inside a Massachusetts power utility for months before discovery. Detection timelines remain a critical gap — intrusion and discovery windows are measured in months, not days.

⇥ Stable / Watch

Hacktivist and nation-state convergence

GRU-funded tooling flowing to hacktivist groups (CARR, Z-Pentest) blurs the attribution line between state operations and plausibly deniable proxy activity. This convergence complicates response decisions and creates attribution ambiguity that adversaries deliberately exploit.

Analytical Assessment

Overall risk trajectory High Confidence

The threat to US critical infrastructure is increasing across all measured dimensions — actor count, operational tempo, sector breadth, and potential physical consequence. The shift from espionage-dominant to disruption-capable operations by PRC actors, combined with Iran's demonstrated willingness to directly manipulate OT systems, represents a materially different risk environment than existed three years prior.

Most significant near-term risk Moderate-High Confidence

Iranian-affiliated actors present the highest near-term disruption risk, particularly as geopolitical tensions remain elevated. Their targeting of internet-exposed OT systems with low-sophistication but high-impact TTPs means that organizations with unpatched, internet-facing PLCs and default credentials remain at acute risk regardless of sector.

Strategic assessment High Confidence

PRC pre-positioning activity (Volt Typhoon, Salt Typhoon) represents the most significant long-term strategic risk. The combination of long-dwell persistence, LOTL tradecraft, and assessed intent to activate capabilities during a future crisis means that organizations may already be compromised without awareness. Detection investment — particularly anomalous lateral movement and LOTL behavior — should be prioritized over perimeter-focused controls.
